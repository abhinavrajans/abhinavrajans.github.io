About

Researcher in Solar Thermal Energy & AI-Driven Energy Systems

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow in the Department of Mechanical and Aeronautical Engineering at the University of Pretoria, South Africa, working on a CFD framework and the implementation of AI — particularly deep learning — for advanced energy systems.

I hold a PhD in Mechanical Engineering from IIT Madras, specialising in renewable energy systems, particularly solar thermal technologies and thermal energy storage, supervised by Prof K. S. Reddy. My doctoral research focused on the "Investigations of Parabolic Dish Collector with Thermal Energy Storage for Process Heating Applications," including the first published application of deep learning to predict the performance of concentrating solar collectors.

Following my PhD, I worked at IIT Patna on an IRDE/DRDO-sponsored project developing thermally controlled modules of optronic payload for stratospheric applications. With over seven years of research experience, I bring deep expertise in designing and optimising advanced energy systems for sustainable applications.

J9
Optical modeling of corrugation cavity receiver for large-aperture solar parabolic dish collector
Rajan, A. & Reddy, K. S.
2022
Energy Sources, Part A, 44(2), 3330–3348· IF 2.9· IIT Madras· DOI
This article presents an optical investigation of a corrugation cavity receiver for a 100 m² parabolic dish collector (PDC). The performance of PDC depends on the selection of the receiver and its mounting distance from the collector base (H) to receive a high amount of reflected radiation. This work performs optical modeling for PDC with a designed corrugation cavity receiver using commercial software, ASAP® 2013. The motivation for this study is derived from the fact that the entrapped internal reflection within the receiver optimizes the system's optical efficiency. The influential geometrical and optical parameters of the receiver have been optimized by varying the aperture diameter, surface absorptivity, receiver coil pitch, and angle of taper. The performance of the optimized receiver is compared with the conical cavity receiver and found to be excellent among all values of H.
